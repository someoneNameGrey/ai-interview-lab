# Behavioral: tell a specific, honest story

Goal: explain your actions and learning without inventing outcomes.

## A useful structure

Situation: what happened? Task: what responsibility was yours? Action: what did you personally do?
Result: what evidence shows the outcome? Reflection: what would you repeat or change? Keep context
short so there is room for actions and reasoning.

## Worked example (fictional)

"A delivery date conflicted with adding a recovery path. I listed the failure cases with the other
engineer, separated launch-critical cases from follow-up work, and proposed a smaller first release.
The team agreed to that scope. I would verify the outcome with release notes and incident records,
rather than claiming the compromise prevented every failure."

This explains an action and a limit. It does not portray the other person as careless or claim
compromise is always right. Real examples can end badly and still show useful reflection.
Do not copy this fictional story as your own.

## Communicating a concern

Describe the observation, its potential consequence, a specific action, and a way to decide.
For example: "We have not tested a dependency timeout. Can I add that test today and bring the
result to the release decision?" The goal is evidence, not a dramatic claim of danger.

Exercise 1 uses your experience or a labeled hypothetical. Exercise 2 is explicitly hypothetical.
Specificity and honesty matter more than polished language.
