# Discovery and architecture defense

Customer-facing technical work begins with listening. Ask who performs the workflow, what failure costs, where data lives, which actions are allowed, current performance and who owns success. Summarize assumptions and unresolved questions before recommending architecture.

## Worked example
An executive asks for full automation in two weeks. A useful response proposes a staff-assist pilot for one ticket category, defines resolution-quality and permission gates, identifies the CRM dependency, and asks for an accountable policy owner. It neither blindly promises delivery nor rejects the goal without an alternative.

With engineers, explain contracts, failure states and reproducible evidence. With product, explain scope and outcome tradeoffs. With security, show identity/data flow, controls, residual risk and owners. With executives, lead with the business decision, investment, evidence and uncertainty. Keep factual content consistent across audiences.

Defend a decision by naming the strongest alternative and evidence that would change your mind. Report incidents as symptom, impact, evidence, mitigation and proposed action. For behavioral answers separate your contribution from team outcomes; do not invent metrics.

## Evidence gate
Run a 20-minute discovery role-play, a five-minute executive recommendation and a technical defense. Produce a one-page implementation plan with dependencies and rollout gates. Obtain human feedback.
